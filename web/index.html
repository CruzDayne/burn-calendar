These are monthly 0.25° aggregates of the harmonized record, not individual detections. For the raw points, download from NASA FIRMS.

  1. Clean. Low-confidence detections, volcanoes, gas flares and offshore sources are dropped. What’s left is counted per 0.25° cell, per month, per satellite.
  2. Count the watching, too. Terra flew alone for two years, Aqua joined in July 2002, VIIRS grew from one satellite to three. Counts are divided by satellite-time in the month, so adding a satellite doesn’t read as more fire.
  3. Put MODIS on VIIRS’s scale. Over 2012–2019, when both were flying and before Terra and Aqua began to drift, we fit a ratio per 5° tile and calendar month. Sparse tiles are pulled toward the global ratio so they don’t swing.
  4. Splice. Calibrated MODIS up to January 2012, observed VIIRS 375 m from February 2012 on.
  5. Test it on held-out years. Leave one overlap year out, predict its VIIRS counts from MODIS, score the miss.

Sources. Hotspots: NASA FIRMS, MODIS Collection 6.1 (Terra, Aqua) and VIIRS 375 m (S-NPP, NOAA-20, NOAA-21), archive and near-real-time files. Coastlines: Natural Earth. Built for the NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026.

A hotspot is a thermal anomaly, not a burned acre. Counts mix how many fires, how big and how hot they are with cloud cover and overpass time. Pre-2012 values are calibrated estimates. Month-to-date figures are compared against the median pro-rated to the days elapsed, which assumes fire is spread evenly through the month.
